What it is, in one paragraph

The portal is the single place our team opens every morning. Employees take jobs from a queue and log the work. Admins watch aging invoices, approve outgoing client emails, review knowledge articles before they become the company answer, and run monthly billing. Clients never log in themselves — they see the portal indirectly, through the receipts, reminders, and manual emails it sends on our behalf. Every mutation writes an audit row. Every new capability sits behind a switch we control.

Compliance posture — enforced in code, not on paper

Receipts: three gates, all must be true

No receipt PDF renders clean until every one of these is on. Any single one being off keeps the DRAFT watermark on every page and blocks finalize.

Gate 1 · Env var
ALLOW_PRODUCTION_ISSUANCE=true on the server itself. Ops-level.
Gate 2 · Flag
receipt_finalize_enabled=true in the database. Admin-controlled.
Gate 3 · Flag
pdf_watermark_disabled=true confirms a real accountant reviewed the templates.
const clean = await isCleanProductionIssuance(); const watermarked = !clean;
lib/pdf/render.ts · lines 112–113 — every PDF renders watermarked unless all three pass

The guardrails that stay on

Nothing on the roadmap loosens any of these. They are the difference between an internal system that can move fast and one that could embarrass us with a client.

  • No real client emails go out in Wave 2. The transport throws even if the flags are flipped.
  • No production receipts finalized. The DRAFT watermark stays on every PDF until a real accountant signs off on templates and numbering.
  • Every billing mutation is admin-only. Employees see billing; they can't change it.
  • Every mutation writes an audit row in the same transaction as the change — not after, not later, not maybe.
  • Every new module ships behind a feature flag that defaults off.
  • Every rate-limited surface has a rate limit — the manual-contact dialog is 30 sends per 15 minutes per IP.
  • Fail-loud on unsupported jurisdiction: the country profile throws if a non-Israel country is passed. No silent defaults.
  • Migrations are forward-only and committed to git. The May debug sweep proved why: one missing migration is one production outage.
